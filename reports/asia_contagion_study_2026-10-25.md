Reading: the claim splits in two, and each half grades differently.

1. "India has fallen less than Korea/Japan/China" (2026): TRUE vs Korea
   (KOSPI -39.1% vs SENSEX -14.7%) and Hong Kong (-17.4%), FALSE vs
   Japan (Nikkei -13.4% has fallen less than India). One of the three
   named neighbours already contradicts the generalisation.

2. "Our markets crash after our neighbours do": the crash CO-MOVEMENT is
   real - after a neighbour crash-cluster trough, India's worst
   drawdown within ~6 months ran -26.5..-29.3% vs -21.4% at random
   dates (KOSPI p=0.012, Nikkei p=0.068, HSI p=0.064), and India was
   itself <= -15% down within 126 sessions after ~70% of neighbour
   troughs (vs ~51% at random, p=0.066-0.133). But the TIMING claim
   fails: weekly neighbour returns have essentially zero correlation
   with India's NEXT week (r=+0.02..+0.05, the same as India's own
   +1-week autocorrelation), while contemporaneous weekly r is
   +0.41..+0.46. Neighbours crash WITH India (near-simultaneous
   troughs; typical lag 0-30 days, sometimes negative), not before it
   in any tradeable way. The 2008 case the video cites fits: KOSPI
   entered its bear months earlier, but both markets troughed within
   24-27 days of each other in late Oct 2008.

   Caveats: crash clusters bunch in crisis periods, so anchors overlap
   and the nominal p-values overstate independence; with three
   neighbours tested, only the KOSPI worst-dd result survives even a
   3-test correction. Read the co-movement as one global risk factor,
   not a lead-lag signal - consistent with the video's fear-propagation
   framing, but useless as a timing rule for India.
