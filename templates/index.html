      📚 Sarvatobhadra Chakra Methodology & Mathematical Rules ▼

      The Sarvatobhadra Chakra is a 9×9 (81-cell) transit diagram of Vedic astrology. The 28 nakshatras (the 27 lunar mansions plus Abhijit) line the border — Krittika starts the East side and the ring runs clockwise. Inside sit the 16 vowels, the 20 name consonants (avakahada), the 12 rashis and the five tithi groups with their weekday rulers.

      Each transiting graha is placed on the nakshatra it occupies and casts vedha (piercing) rays across the chakra: front (straight across), left (diagonal in the direction of motion) and right (the other diagonal). The classical school uses one operative ray per graha chosen by its motion — swift casts left, retrograde casts right, mean speed casts front; Sun and Moon always cast left, Rahu and Ketu always right.

      From the market’s anchor nakshatra nine sensitive stars are counted out on the 28-star ring; the five Pancha-Shakha points (Janma, Karma, Sanghatika, Samudayika, Adhana) form the market core. A malefic (Saturn, Sun, Mars, Rahu, Ketu, ashubha Moon or harsh Mercury) vedha on a core point is bearish; a benefic (Jupiter, Venus, shubha Moon or well-placed Mercury) vedha is bullish. Strength is scaled by dignity — retrograde ×2, exalted ×3, debilitated ×0.5, combust ×0.5.

      The Rasi Sector Projection panel nets the same vedha rays over the twelve rashis of the inner ring: every ray that pierces a rashi cell adds the piercing graha’s signed strength to that rashi. Each rashi is mapped to the market sectors classically associated with it (banks for Taurus, oil & gas for Scorpio, technology for Aquarius, etc.); a negative net is a bearish projection, a positive net bullish, and a rashi left untouched by any vedha shows no signal.

      Sources: Phaladeepika XXVI, Horaratna/Rajavijaya (preserved in the Phaladeepika XXVI.48 commentary), Narapatijayacharya; layout verified against classical references.

5. How the score works
•	Each day's planetary positions are computed (11:30 IST; sidereal Lahiri; mean Rahu/Ketu). The Venus-in-11th tests use the Mumbai 09:15 IST opening-bell chart with whole-sign houses.
•	Each active combination adds its weight. Weight = (historical lift − 1) × n/(n+5), where lift compares how often the combination appeared on 26 crash days (1929–2025) with how often it appears on any weekday since 1925, and n is the number of crash days it appeared on. Combinations seen in only a few crashes are discounted.
•	ELEVATED = score ≥ 1.85 (about the top 10% of all days since 1925). HIGH = score ≥ 2.36 (about the top 3%). Flagged days less than 4 days apart are joined into one period.
•	Historically, about 42% of crash days scored ELEVATED or higher and about 23% scored HIGH, against 10% and 3% of ordinary days. These figures are in-sample (the weights come from the same crash days), so expect weaker results going forward. Most flagged periods will not contain a crash, and many crashes occur outside flagged periods.
•	None of the combinations passed a strict multiple-testing correction in the research. Use the windows as a risk-management prompt (position size, stops, hedges) alongside technical and macro confirmation — not as a sell signal.
