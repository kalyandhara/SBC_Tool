
1. Core Philosophy: Astrology & Market Psychology
The instructor begins with a metaphor about a "hot-tempered man" visiting his in-laws' house; just as a person suppresses anger in a restrictive environment, planetary aggression (like Mars) needs a specific "platform" to manifest. If that platform isn't present, aggression turns into "hidden anger." He applies this to market movements, stating that "Bull" and "Bear" planets also need specific conditions (platforms) to perform.

2. Market Predictions & Analysis
2026 Outlook: The instructor predicts significant volatility and potential wars in 2026, suggesting the market could drop 10-20% from its highs.
Current Trend: He identifies a Bearish phase from December 21st lasting until March 21st. He advises retail investors to keep funds in Fixed Deposits (FDs) for a few months and buy ETFs/Mutual Funds only after the market corrects in March.
FII vs. DII: He notes that Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) have been selling for months, while Domestic Mutual Funds are buying primarily due to compliance and money flow.

3. Commodity Analysis (Gold & Silver)
Gold Rally: He explains the current gold rally using the Krittika Nakshatra (associated with gold). By analyzing the distance between the planetary positions of Jupiter, Saturn, and the Aries sign (Mesh), he argues that gold has been in a "one-way rally" since mid-August.
Gold vs. Silver: While gold is driven by fear and global instability, silver is more volatile. He suggests booking profits if one has significant gains.

4. The "Nakshatra" Formula for Commodities
The instructor introduces a specific system for predicting commodity trends based on Sankranti (the sun's entry into a new sign) and Nakshatras (lunar mansions):

Nakshatra Categories: He divides the 27 Nakshatras into three sizes: Brihat (Large), Sam (Medium), and Jaganya (Small).
The Formula: If the Moon is in a "Brihat" Nakshatra during Sankranti, it generally signals a bullish trend.
Specific Example: During the January 14th Sankranti, the Moon is in Anuradha Nakshatra. Since Anuradha represents rice, wheat, and pulses, he predicts a 3-5% price fluctuation or trend reversal in these agriculture commodities between mid-January and mid-February.

5. Impact of Eclipses (Grahan)
He explains that an eclipse "damages" the Nakshatra in which it occurs, leading to scarcity and subsequent price rises in related commodities:

February 17, 2026: Eclipse in Dhanishta Nakshatra. This Nakshatra governs precious metals (other than gold/silver), foreign currencies, and intangible currencies (Crypto). He predicts significant instability in the currency market and suggests the Indian Rupee (INR) may depreciate further against the Dollar.
March 3, 2026: Eclipse in Purva Phalguni Nakshatra. This may impact silver and specific regions like Southeast Asia and parts of India.

6. Trading Discipline & Risk Management
The instructor emphasizes a strict "Trader's Code":

Fixed Exits: Before taking a trade, one must write down the entry, stop-loss, and target on paper and stick to them without exception.
Capital Preservation: He stresses that "Capital is King." He shares a story of a successful student who uses only the interest from a ₹1 Crore FD to trade options, ensuring the principal remains safe.
The 1% Rule: He recommends risking no more than 1% of total cash in hand for option trades.
